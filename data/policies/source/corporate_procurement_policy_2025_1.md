# Corporate Procurement Policy

**Document ID:** CORP_PROCUREMENT_2025_1  
**Version:** 2025.1  
**Effective:** 2025-01-01  
**Expires:** 2025-12-31

## Section 1 Purpose, Scope, and Governance

This policy establishes common procurement governance for enterprise purchases. It applies to employees who request, approve, select, or administer suppliers. It sets decision principles; the Approval Authority Matrix and Contract Compliance Policy contain the authoritative approval and contract requirements.

Procurement records must identify the business need, supplier, category, committed amount, and accountable approver. Purchase commitments are made only through the approved procurement process.

## Section 2 Purchase Governance

Business owners remain responsible for a clear need, an appropriate supplier recommendation, and a complete purchase record. Procurement may request supporting market, qualification, commercial, or contract information before a commitment is issued.

This policy does not create an automated compliance threshold for competitive sourcing. Approval thresholds and timing requirements are governed by the applicable Approval Authority Matrix.

## Section 3 Competitive Sourcing Guidance

For material purchases, teams should consider competitive alternatives when practical and document the basis for their recommendation. The number of quotations, commercial comparison, and sourcing rationale are human review evidence in this MVP; they are not automatic PASS or FAIL conditions.

## Section 4 Supplier Selection Principles

Supplier selection should consider capability, qualifications, commercial value, continuity of supply, and category risk. Critical-category concentration and limited alternatives should trigger mitigation discussion, not an automatic policy violation.

## Section 5 Exceptions and Related Policies

Documented exceptions must be reviewed under the policy that governs the exception type. Contract exceptions are governed by the Contract Compliance Policy. Approval requirements are governed by the Approval Authority Matrix. Where policies conflict, the more specific applicable policy controls.
